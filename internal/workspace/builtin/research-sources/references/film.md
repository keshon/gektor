# Film research

Start with the title, release year, and cut or adaptation when they matter.
For a character question, add the character name and look up the film and any
source work separately.

| Source | Use it for | How to search |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/) | Plot orientation, characters, adaptations, and a trail to cited interviews or production sources | Use `search_web` with `engine: wikipedia` and the film title. Add the year or search the character separately if several works share a name. |
| [AFI Catalog](https://catalog.afi.com/) | American film credits, release information, synopses, and production notes | Search the film title in the catalog. Use the release year to distinguish records. |
| [Margaret Herrick Library script collection](https://www.oscars.org/margaret-herrick-library/collections/scripts) | Identifying screenplays, treatments, drafts, and cutting continuities | Follow the link to Academy Collections and search title plus screenwriter. The collection lists holdings, and scripts are available in the reading room. |
| Studio, distributor, filmmaker, or public archive | Released screenplays, press kits, interviews, and commentary | Search the exact title and year with `screenplay PDF`, `press kit`, or the filmmaker's name. Prefer files published by a named rights holder, writer, or archive. |

Label the evidence by version. A screenplay draft, the theatrical cut, and
a later cut can differ. For exact dialogue, identify whether the wording
comes from a released version or a dated draft. For questions about meaning,
distinguish what happens on screen from a creator's stated intent and your
interpretation.
